Understanding Body Mass Index (BMI)

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a standardized screening measurement created by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet. It provides a simple ratio of weight to squared height to help classify individuals into broad nutritional categories.

Metric Formula:
• BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m)]²

Imperial Formula:
• BMI = [Weight (lbs) × 703] ÷ [Height (inches)]²

Standard WHO vs. Asian-Indian Classifications

Because physiological body fat distribution varies across populations, health organizations apply adjusted thresholds:

Category WHO International Standard Asian-Indian Standard (ICMR) Associated Health Risk
Underweight < 18.5 < 18.5 Nutritional deficiency, lower immunity
Normal Weight 18.5 – 24.9 18.5 – 22.9 Lowest cardiovascular & metabolic risk
Overweight 25.0 – 29.9 23.0 – 24.9 Moderate risk of diabetes & hypertension
Obese ≥ 30.0 ≥ 25.0 Substantially elevated metabolic risk

Important Clinical Limitations of BMI

BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis: It does not distinguish lean skeletal muscle mass from adipose fat tissue, nor does it measure visceral abdominal fat. Athletes, pregnant women, and the elderly should consult a physician for comprehensive body composition testing (such as DEXA scans or waist-to-hip ratio measurements).
