How GST Calculation Works

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a destination-based, multi-stage indirect tax implemented in India in July 2017 to replace many earlier indirect taxes such as VAT, excise duty and service tax.

When generating an invoice or purchasing goods, GST can be applied in two directions:

  • Exclusive GST (Add Tax): Starting with a net wholesale or base manufacturing cost and adding statutory tax to calculate the customer billing amount.
  • Inclusive GST (Extract Tax): Starting with a maximum retail price (MRP) or final gross bill and removing the built-in tax to reveal the true underlying merchant cost.
Adding GST (Exclusive):
• GST Amount = (Base Amount × GST Rate) ÷ 100
• Gross Total = Base Amount + GST Amount

Removing GST (Inclusive):
• Base Amount = (Gross Total × 100) ÷ (100 + GST Rate)
• GST Amount = Gross Total − Base Amount Intra-State Split: CGST = GST ÷ 2  |  SGST = GST ÷ 2
Inter-State Transaction: IGST = Full GST Amount

Worked Example: Adding & Removing 18% GST

Case A: Adding 18% GST to ₹1,000 (Exclusive)

  1. Base price = ₹1,000
  2. GST = (1000 × 18) / 100 = ₹180
  3. Total invoice value = ₹1,000 + ₹180 = ₹1,180
  4. Tax breakdown: CGST (9%) = ₹90, SGST (9%) = ₹90

Case B: Extracting 18% GST from ₹1,180 (Inclusive MRP)

  1. Gross total = ₹1,180
  2. Base price = (1,180 × 100) / (100 + 18) = 118000 / 118 = ₹1,000
  3. Embedded GST = ₹1,180 - ₹1,000 = ₹180

Current Indian GST Rate Slabs (from 22 September 2025)

The GST Council's 56th meeting restructured the rate slabs, and the new rates took effect on 22 September 2025. The main slabs are now 5% and 18%, plus a 40% rate for luxury and sin goods and a 0% (nil) rate for certain items. The earlier 12% and 28% slabs no longer exist. The examples below are only illustrations of what reporting on the reform describes — the rate for any specific product or service depends on its HSN/SAC classification and the official notification.

Tax Slab Examples reported for the slab CGST + SGST Split (intra-state)
0% (Nil) Certain essentials, including life-saving medicines and individual health/life insurance premiums. 0% + 0%
5% Many everyday goods such as butter, ghee, cheese, packaged snacks, shampoo and toothpaste. 2.5% + 2.5%
18% The standard rate for most other goods and services, including small cars and many consumer appliances. 9% + 9%
40% Luxury and sin goods such as tobacco products, aerated beverages and high-end cars. 20% + 20%

Rates are changed by the GST Council and the government, so check the latest CBIC/GST portal notification before relying on a rate for filing or invoicing.

What This Calculator Does Not Cover

  • Which rate applies. The calculator applies the rate you choose. It does not look up the rate for a product or service (that depends on its HSN/SAC code).
  • Intra-state or inter-state. The result shows both the CGST + SGST split and the IGST amount. Use only the one that matches your sale: CGST and SGST for supplies within one state, IGST for supplies between states. Both total the same tax.
  • Other levies and rules. Cess (if applicable to a product), composition scheme, reverse charge, input tax credit and invoice-level rounding are not included.
  • Rounding. Amounts are shown to two decimals, so on very small amounts CGST + SGST may differ from the total by one paisa.

Frequently Asked Questions

To add GST: GST Amount = (Base Price × GST Rate) / 100. Total Price = Base Price + GST Amount. To remove GST from an inclusive price: Base Price = (Gross Price × 100) / (100 + GST Rate).

Since 22 September 2025 (GST 2.0), most goods and services fall under 5% or 18%, with a 40% rate for luxury and sin goods and a 0% (nil) rate for certain items. The old 12% and 28% slabs were removed. Always confirm the rate for a specific item or service in the official GST notification or with a tax professional.

For intra-state transactions (within the same state), GST is split equally into Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST). For inter-state transactions (between two different states), Integrated GST (IGST) is collected by the Central Government.
